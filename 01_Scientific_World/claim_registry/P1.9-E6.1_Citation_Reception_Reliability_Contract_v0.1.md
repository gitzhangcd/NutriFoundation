## Core scientific separation

Scientific reception must answer three different questions:

1. What did the original evidence show?
2. How visible/influential did the source or claim become?
3. How reliable is the claim now after independent replication, criticism, corrections and later evidence?

Normative invariants:

- Journal Impact ≠ Article Validity
- Citation Count ≠ Support Count
- Supportive Citation ≠ Independent Replication
- Guideline Uptake ≠ Independent Causal Evidence
- Scientific Influence ≠ Claim Reliability

## New objects

- CitationReceptionRecord
- ScientificInfluenceSignal
- ClaimReliabilitySignal

These objects are sidecars. They do not overwrite SourceArtifact, EvidenceUnit or ScientificClaim lineage.

## Citation reception taxonomy

Each claim-aligned citation context should be classified as:

- supports
- contrasts
- contradicts
- independent_replication
- failed_replication
- methodological_use
- background_mention
- extends_generalizes
- critique

Claim alignment must be one of exact_claim, partial_claim, background_only, or unclear.

Only exact/partial claim-aligned contexts may affect reliability. Background-only mentions remain influence signals.

## Independent replication contract

A later publication counts as an independent replication only when it evaluates a sufficiently aligned claim with new empirical data and is not merely a reuse of the same cohort, trial follow-up, review evidence set or derivative guideline lineage.

Replication classes:

- direct_replication
- conceptual_replication
- null_replication
- failed_replication
- not_comparable

Every reception analysis must retain independence_group_id and probable derivative lineage.

## Evidence-family de-duplication

Primary study → systematic review → guideline → many guideline citations does not equal many independent confirmations.

Before aggregation, the Agent must identify evidence families, shared cohorts, shared trials and review/guideline dependency.

## Scientific influence contract

ScientificInfluenceSignal may include:

- raw citation count
- field/year-normalized citation percentile
- citation velocity
- systematic-review uptake
- meta-analysis uptake
- guideline/consensus uptake
- number of independent citing groups
- optional journal-level metrics when legally/technically available

Journal Impact Factor or other journal metrics are visibility priors only. They must not determine claim truth, replace methodological assessment, upgrade observational evidence to causal evidence, override failed replication/correction/retraction, or be guessed when unavailable.

## Claim reliability model

No single weighted scalar is normative in v0.1.

For claim C at time t:

R_t(C) = <Intrinsic Validity, Independence, Replication, Reception, Integrity, Temporal Maturity, Applicability Stability>

Scientific influence is represented separately:

Inf_t(C) = <Citations, Field-normalized citations, Velocity, Guideline uptake, Synthesis uptake, Journal visibility>

Inf_t(C) does not imply R_t(C).

Allowed reliability states:

- insufficient
- provisional
- convergent
- robust
- contested
- degraded
- invalidated

## Upgrade and downgrade logic

Potential upgrades: independent direct replication, convergence across independent datasets, concordant high-quality synthesis, stable claim-level reception, and absence of major integrity events.

Mandatory escalation: failed replication, claim-aligned contradiction, major methodological critique, result-changing correction, expression of concern, retraction, newly discovered evidence dependency, or material scope narrowing.

## Citation-context confidence

Operational gates:

- confidence ≥0.90: automatic candidate classification allowed
- 0.70–0.89: candidate + secondary Agent verification
- <0.70: human escalation

Regardless of classifier confidence, contradiction, failed replication, retraction/correction with claim impact, major critique, and ambiguous high-impact claim alignment require human/expert review before Claim-Gold.

## Temporal versioning

CitationReception_t is not CitationReception_(t+1). Every snapshot must retain date, provider, retrieval/query method, coverage limitations and version. Historical snapshots are immutable.

## Responsible metrics policy

Quantitative indicators must be contextual. Field-normalized citation signals are preferred to raw counts when available. Provider coverage differences must be recorded. Journal metrics are contextual visibility indicators only.

## Non-regression rules

- Citation ≠ EvidenceUnit
- Influence ≠ Reliability
- Reception ≠ Recommendation
- Replication ≠ Applicability
- observational association cannot become causal because it is highly cited
- guideline adoption cannot be double-counted as independent causal support

## E7 integration

ScientificClaim_candidate + CitationReceptionRecord + ScientificInfluenceSignal + ClaimReliabilitySignal → E7 Qualification → Human/Expert Adjudication → ScientificClaim_GOLD.

No Agent may silently rewrite a ScientificClaim because its citation count changes.
