## SC-B001-C01 result

Reliability state: **robust**.

Key reception findings:

- Finnish DPS long-term follow-up preserved a significant diabetes-prevention effect after the active intervention period.
- DPPOS showed a persistent, attenuated diabetes-incidence reduction over 15 years.
- Independent Indian IDPP-1 and Japanese IGT randomized trials replicated prevention effects in distinct populations.
- A Japanese IFG trial extended the finding but showed that effect strength depended on baseline dysglycemia severity.
- Systematic-review reception remained supportive, while emphasizing heterogeneity of interventions and comparators.

Independence judgment:

- DPS original + DPS follow-ups = one evidence family.
- DPP original + DPPOS = one evidence family.
- India and Japan trials count as independent replication families.

Influence snapshot:

- DPP primary: 18,132 citations in the Metascience Observatory data-through-Jan-2025 snapshot.
- Finnish DPS primary: 11,028 citations in the same provider snapshot.
- DPPOS 15-year follow-up: 850 citations in the provider snapshot.
- Finnish sustained follow-up: 1,744 citations in the provider snapshot.

These values are influence signals only. The reliability upgrade is driven by independent randomized replication and longitudinal persistence, not citation volume.

## SC-B001-C05 result

Reliability state: **convergent**.

Key reception findings:

- A dedicated Look AHEAD review states that weight loss and fitness improved but cardiovascular outcomes did not, while many other health benefits were observed.
- A broader T2D cardiovascular-trial overview similarly cautions that improvement in traditional risk factors does not guarantee cardiovascular outcome benefit.
- A later cardiovascular-outcome meta-analysis across heterogeneous glucose-lowering strategies shows intervention-specific hard-outcome benefit, which limits any overbroad interpretation that lifestyle/weight loss can never benefit cardiovascular outcomes.
- A later Look AHEAD weight-variability analysis reuses the same cohort and is not independent replication of the randomized MACE contrast.

Influence snapshot:

- NEJM publisher metrics retrieved in this run report 2,548 citations for the Look AHEAD cardiovascular outcomes paper.
- The same publisher page reports references in 31 clinical-guideline sources and 8 policy sources.

Integrity escalation:

- The NEJM publisher page marks the Look AHEAD article as corrected.
- The correction content was not resolved in this execution and must be checked before Claim-Gold.

## SC-B001-C02 result

Reliability state: **convergent**, with mixed reception.

Supporting reception:

- Large long-term low-fat meta-analysis: low-fat was not superior to equally intensive alternative diets; comparator intensity mattered.
- 2022 low-carb vs low-fat meta-analysis: modest low-carb advantages at 6–23 months, no difference after 24 months.
- T2D umbrella review: no particular macronutrient profile was generally superior; energy-restriction/TDR intensity mattered.
- 2023 T2D network meta-analysis: short-term differences occurred, but evidence was insufficient to select one optimal dietary pattern.
- ADA consensus: no one-size-fits-all eating plan or ideal macronutrient percentage applies broadly.

Meaningful contrast:

- A 2024 network meta-analysis of 7 RCTs/9 trials ranked low-carbohydrate highest for weight loss/body fat in its specific network, while acknowledging effect variation by age, sex, genetics and lifestyle.

This is preserved as a scope-limited contrast, not discarded. It blocks an overbroad Gold claim and requires E7 adjudication.

Influence snapshot:

- POUNDS LOST: 1,881 citations in Metascience data through Jan 2025.
- A TO Z: 1,030 citations in the same provider snapshot.
- DIETFITS: 590 citations in the same provider snapshot.

Integrity note:

- DIETFITS has documented 2018 corrections to lipid units/conversion instructions and funding description; the publisher/PMC record does not describe a change to the primary weight-loss or genotype/INS-30 conclusions.
